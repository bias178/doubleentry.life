Like for Like: One Quarter, Four Growth Rates

One illustrative company, round numbers, and four answers to how much it grew.

Take a company with revenue of 1,000 in a quarter last year and 940 in the same quarter this year. This is an illustrative case with round numbers, no real company behind it.

The income statement says revenue fell 6.0%. The results release says it grew 3.5%. A slide in the investor presentation says 6.3%. All three describe the same quarter of the same company.

Last year's 1,000 includes 150 from a business sold in the meantime. It also includes 50 from a business in a market now under sanctions.

This year's 940 includes 40 of transitory revenue, services the company still provides to the buyer while the separation is completed. It includes 20 that come only from a favourable exchange rate. And it includes 30 from the business in the sanctioned market.

Now take things out one at a time.

Remove the business that was sold and the transitory revenue. Last year 850, this year 900. Growth is 5.9%.

Remove the exchange rate effect. 850 against 880. Growth is 3.5%. This is the figure the release calls like for like.

Remove the sanctioned business from both years. 800 against 850. Growth is 6.3%.

Each step comes with a reason. A business that was sold no longer exists, so comparing against it makes no sense. Exchange rates are outside management's control. A sanctioned market cannot be served. Every reason is fair, and the last two are still decisions.

Like for like is not defined by IFRS. It is a measure each company defines for itself, normally in the appendix of the results release, together with a bridge to the reported figure.

The same logic works over a full year. In one annual report I read, reported net sales fell 5% and organic net sales fell 2%. Between the two sit currency (plus 1 point), disposals and an acquisition (minus 6 points) and a 53rd week (plus 2 points). The bridge adds up: minus 2, plus 1, minus 6, plus 2, equals minus 5.

The headline number depends on a perimeter the company chose. The bridge back to the reported figure is in the appendix, and it is worth reading before the headline.

Sources

Illustrative case, round numbers, no real company. Like for like is an alternative performance measure, defined by each company. The full year example is read in a published annual report, figures rounded.
