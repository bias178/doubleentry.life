EP.35 FY 2026 Prepaid Expense 17 Nov 2026

A Black Friday deal: a year of streaming for 70 euro. Bill's ledger keeps count, month by month.

November 2026. The offer halves the annual plan of a video streaming service. The money leaves his account on day one, and the ledger spreads it over 12 months, so the part not yet used stays visible.

On 17 November Bill signs up for a video streaming service. The Black Friday offer, which runs for the whole of November, takes the annual plan from 140 euro to 70. The monthly plan costs 14, so 12 months at that rate would cost 168.

He pays the 70 euro that day, all at once, so his account is 70 euro lighter on 17 November. The ledger does something else with the same money: it divides it by 12 and charges him about 6 euro a month, which is what the service costs him month by month, whatever the bank statement says. The part not yet charged stays on the books as months already paid for and not yet used.

📖What accounting says

A payment and a cost are two events, and a cost belongs to the months in which the service is used. That is why 6 euro a month, and not 70 euro at once, is the right figure for the ledger. What has been paid and not yet used stays on the balance sheet as an asset, the right to the months still to come. Accountants call it a prepaid expense.

On the day he pays, Bill's net assets do not move: cash goes down by 70 and the prepaid expense goes up by 70. November's profit will carry 6 euro of the plan, and the rest will arrive month by month.

🔍In real life

The plan renews in November 2027. The 70 euro is an introductory price, the usual price of the annual plan is 140, and at renewal Bill will almost certainly pay more than he pays now. By then he will have a year of habit behind the service, and the renewal date is when that habit gets tested.

The annual plan also removes the monthly charge that would have kept reminding him it exists. One payment appears on the bank statement once and then not again for a year. Bill has a method for this. At the closing of every year he goes through all his subscriptions and measures each one against how much he used it. The last closing, in January, took a streaming service out of his costs.

The ledger covers the rest. The prepaid expense falls by 6 euro every month, so the plan keeps a balance that anyone can read. At the end of December it will stand at 58 euro.

📒In the ledger

The Conceptual Framework defines an expense as a decrease in assets, and the prepaid expense is an asset until the service is used. IAS 1.66 classifies an asset as current when it is expected to be realised within twelve months after the reporting period. At 31 December 2026 the 58 euro left covers January to October 2027, so all of it is current.

70 divided by 12 is 5.83, so the ledger posts 6 a month and the last month 4, as it does for the certification. The plan runs from November 2026 to October 2027: 12 euro goes to FY 2026 (November and December) and 58 euro to FY 2027. November closes with the next episode, so on 17 November only the first entry is posted.

The tip

An annual plan has two prices: the one paid today and the one it renews at. Divide the payment by 12 to get the real monthly cost, then write the renewal date and the renewal price next to it.

Prepaid Expense

A prepayment buys months, and the ledger uses them up one at a time. Bill pays 70 euro on 17 November for a year of a video streaming service. The ledger charges him about 6 euro a month, so on the day the whole 70 euro stands on the balance sheet as a prepaid expense and falls by 6 every month. Net assets do not move: they stand at 7,247. The cost reaches the books once the months are used.
